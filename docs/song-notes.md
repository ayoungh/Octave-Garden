# Beginner song notes

All seven songs have Learn, Listen and Try it stages and are available in Play.
These are newly entered, single-line beginner arrangements, with no lyrics,
commercial recordings, accompaniments or downloaded score artwork bundled.
The four additions use C–G with fixed right-hand fingers 1–5.

| Addition | Practice focus | Arrangement scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Au clair de la lune | Three repeated Cs and two-beat notes | Opening melody repeated; phrase-end rests replaced by held notes |
| Lightly Row | Skips and stepwise motion | Opening eight bars |
| Jingle Bells | Repeated notes and contrasting endings | Chorus; simplified articulation |
| When the Saints Go Marching In | C–E skip, long notes and repeated phrases | Familiar melody with simplified steady rhythm |

Historical and melody references consulted:

- [Au clair de la lune in Chansons et rondes enfantines](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Chansons_et_rondes_enfantines/Au_clair_de_la_lune): opening melody, transposed to C.
- [Lightly Row melody reference](https://abcnotation.com/tunePage?a=trillian.mit.edu%2F~jc%2Fmusic%2Fabc%2Fmirror%2Fmusicaviva.com%2Fgermany%2Flightly-row%2F0000): pitch and duration cross-check for the opening.
- [The One Horse Open Sleigh, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2023812429/): James Lord Pierpont and the historical Jingle Bells publication.
- [When the Saints Go Marching In, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc9999005.3748): traditional-music catalogue reference. The app uses a simple melody arrangement, not a transcription of the Armstrong recording.

In untimed practice, correct note order advances the lesson. In Play, the same
steps supply the falling-note chart and timing targets. Neither mode checks the
physical finger used; Play judges attack timing rather than held-note duration.
